There is a stage in learning security where everything looks like a wall. The
tooling assumes you already know the protocol, the protocol assumes you already
know the network, and the write-ups skip the four hours where nothing worked.

## The write-ups lie by omission

Not deliberately. It is just that "I misread the port number for twenty
minutes" is not interesting to write down. So the published version of every
engagement looks like a straight line, and your version looks like a mess.
Both are the same work.

## Depth compounds, breadth does not

Tools change. The reason a tool works does not. An afternoon spent
understanding how a session cookie is actually validated pays for itself for
years; an afternoon spent memorising flags pays for itself until the next major
version.

## Write things down before you understand them

The notes I get the most value from are the ones written while I was still
confused. They record the wrong model I had at the time, which is the only way
to notice later exactly which assumption was wrong.

## Rest is part of the method

The bug I cannot find at 1am is usually obvious at 9am. That is not motivation
advice, it is just how attention works — and the sooner you plan around it, the
less time you lose to it.
